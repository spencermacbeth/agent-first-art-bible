# A save-point terminal pass

This is a fictional example of how the records relate. The candidates, feedback and measurements below are illustrative; no generation or device test is claimed.

## Brief and references

A small exploration game needs a save-point terminal that is legible from a fixed elevated camera. Its square footprint and interaction zone already exist. The unresolved question is the terminal's architectural identity.

The seed packet contains `ref-bracket-01`, the developer's sketch of a hanging bracket, and `ref-stone-02`, their photograph of weathered stone. The sketch contributes suspension and proportion. The photograph contributes broad surface wear; its decorative text should not be copied. Original media remain separate from review crops.

The project permits a concept-only batch within an already approved budget. Reconstruction and device deployment are outside this task. Candidate records name the provider and parameters actually used; this example does not prescribe a model.

## Broad comparison

| ID | Proposed direction | Changes | Fixed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| terminal-a | Suspended instrument | Asymmetric bracket, hanging body, painted metal | Footprint, interaction side, camera and readable activation area |
| terminal-b | Stone archive marker | Low monolith, recessed face, broad wear | Same |
| terminal-c | Fabric signal post | Flexible canopy, exposed ribs, translucent panels | Same |

These candidates investigate different structures. A later comparison could hold the suspended form fixed and vary the surface treatment.

## Review and feedback

The concept taste pass shows all three at the same intended size, with an overlay for the gameplay footprint. Each image has its own candidate ID and content hash. The owner writes:

```text
Keep terminal-a's suspended silhouette. Its polished metal is wrong.
Try a matte finish using the broad wear in ref-stone-02, without making
the object look carved from stone. Leave the hanging proportions alone.
```

The decision accepts the silhouette as a concept reference. Material remains open. It does not approve a mesh, animation or production integration.

## Refinement and lock-in

The next brief points to the exact `terminal-a` artifact, fixes the silhouette and bracket proportions, and explores matte wear. It uses a new candidate identity rather than replacing the approved image. The earlier candidates and notes remain available.

After an explicit material choice, the bible can link the selected concept and its accepted properties. If a later task authorizes reconstruction, it adds separate checks for thin geometry, material appearance and footprint in the production scene. An attractive orbit view alone cannot settle readability through the game's fixed camera.

The runtime stage can remain pending while the concept is authoritative. The next agent can see that boundary in the active bible rather than reconstructing it from this conversation.
